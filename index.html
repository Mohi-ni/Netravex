DPDP Act in Plain Language

A Bank Example in Three Simple Roles

Every day, a co-operative bank handles KYC records, account balances, employee details, and service requests. Understanding who owns the decision versus who processes the data is the foundation of compliance.

01
Individual

Data Principal

The customer or employee whose personal data is collected. They hold statutory rights to notice, access, correction, erasure, and grievance redressal.

Example: Savings account holder updating mobile KYC
02
Decision Owner

Data Fiduciary

The co-operative bank deciding why and how personal data is used. Directly accountable for lawful basis, security safeguards, and breach reporting.

Example: Co-operative bank issuing loans & managing accounts
03
Specialist Vendor

Data Processor

A third-party service provider processing personal data strictly on the bank's documented instructions and contractual safeguards.

Example: Core banking vendor, SMS gateway, or KYC agency

Trust is Built at Every Data Hand-Off

5-stage personal data lifecycle across bank touchpoints

END-TO-END GOVERNANCE
01. COLLECT

Customer applies for account/loan or updates KYC details with plain-language notice.

02. USE

Branch, operations & compliance teams use data strictly for stated purposes.

03. SHARE

Payment networks, auditors, or SMS vendors receive only limited, governed data.

04. STORE

Records sit encrypted in core banking systems, branch files, and disaster backups.

05. DELETE

Securely erased or anonymised once purpose ends and statutory duties expire.

Core Technical Security Controls for Banks

Encryption

Scramble sensitive KYC & account records at rest and in transit.

Data Masking

Hide Aadhaar/PAN digits when full view is not required.

Least Privilege

Role-based access so staff only view records needed for duty.

Activity Logs

Retain immutable audit trails of who accessed what and when.

Safe Backups

Tested recovery procedures to restore services after failure.

Vendor Clauses

Binding DPDP security & breach rules for third-party IT partners.

First 30 Days Roadmap

Practical steps your co-operative bank can take immediately to build a defensible foundation.

Days 1 – 5

Appoint Sponsor & Coordinator

  • Board appoints an executive DPDP sponsor
  • Nominate a working coordinator & cross-functional team
  • Hold kickoff meeting and define system scope
Days 6 – 12

List Systems, Data & Vendors

  • Catalog Core Banking, CRM, branch files & email systems
  • Map personal data types (KYC, transactions, HR)
  • Identify third-party processors & cross-border flows
Days 13 – 20

Review Notices, Rights & Controls

  • Audit customer privacy notices & consent capture
  • Test customer rights & 90-day grievance routing
  • Verify user access permissions, logs, backups & patching
Days 21 – 30

Incident Playbook & Prioritisation

  • Draft red-alert breach contacts & 72-hour escalation list
  • Prioritise high-impact compliance gaps & quick wins
  • Approve a realistic multi-quarter rollout plan
